2. What we do not collect

Our games do not collect, store, transmit, sell, rent, or share any personal information. In particular, they do not collect:

  • Your name, email address, phone number, mailing address, or any account credentials — our games have no sign-up, sign-in, or user account of any kind
  • Device identifiers, the Identifier for Advertisers (IDFA), the Android Advertising ID, or any other advertising or tracking identifier — our games do not use Apple’s App Tracking Transparency framework because they perform no tracking
  • Location data of any kind, precise or coarse
  • Contacts, calendars, or files
  • Camera or microphone input
  • Health, fitness, financial, or purchase information
  • Usage analytics, gameplay telemetry, crash reports, or performance diagnostics
  • Browsing history, search history, or any advertising profile

Because our games collect nothing, there is no personal information for us to lose, misuse, disclose, or hand over to anyone else. The one exception is what you send us yourself: if you choose to email us, we will of course have your message and your email address. Section 13 explains what we do with it.

Song sharing touches your photo library in two narrow ways: it can add a song card to your photos, and it can read the single card picture you choose to import. Neither one gives us access to your photo library, and neither sends anything to us. Section 5 explains this in full.

3. About the microphone

We make music games, so players sometimes ask whether a game listens to them play or sing. It does not. All performance input is made through on-screen touch controls. Our games never request microphone permission, never switch the microphone on, and never record audio from your surroundings or anywhere else. The same is true of the camera and of location services.

4. Data on your device

A game saves your progress locally on your device so you can pick up where you left off. This save file contains only gameplay state, such as:

  • Which songs, levels, or stages you have unlocked
  • Which tutorial steps you have completed, and where you last played
  • Any performances you record in-game, stored as note-and-timing data used to replay your take inside the game
  • Any song a friend sent you that you chose to import — kept alongside the songs you make yourself, together with the performance your friend recorded, also as note-and-timing data

This data is written using the standard local storage provided by your device’s operating system and stays inside the game’s own storage container. It is never uploaded to us or to anyone else, and we have no ability to read it. It is not linked to your identity, and it does not contain any personal information.

Sharing a song also writes the song card as an image file. On iOS that file goes into the app’s temporary cache, which the operating system clears on its own. If you choose to save a card to your Photos, that copy is yours and stays in your photo library until you delete it.

You can erase this data at any time by deleting the game from your device. Note that, like any app’s local storage, it may be included in the device backups you make through iCloud, Google, or your computer. Those backups are handled entirely by the platform that provides them, under that platform’s own privacy policy, and remain inaccessible to us.

5. Sharing songs

You can turn a song you made into a song card — a picture with a QR code on it. A friend adds that song to their own game by opening the picture. Here is exactly what that does.

What is in the code. The QR code holds the song’s name and its notes. That is all it holds. There is nothing about you in it: no name, no account, no device identifier, no location. Do keep in mind that the song name is whatever you typed, so leave personal details out of it if you plan to share the card.

Nothing is uploaded. The code holds the song itself, not a web link. Making a card and reading a card both happen on your device. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is downloaded, and the game does not go online for any part of this.

Photos, when you send. To send a song, you can save the card to your Photos. iOS asks your permission first. That permission lets us add a picture and nothing else — it does not let us read your photos.

Photos, when you receive. To open a song from a friend, you pick the card in the iOS photo picker. That picker runs outside the game, so we never request access to your photo library and never hold it. We receive only the one picture you pick, and we cannot see any other photo.

Sending a card on. The card is an ordinary picture. You can send it any way you would send a picture — a messaging app, email, AirDrop, a social post, Files, or by saving it to your Photos and sending it from there. Whichever way you choose, that service has its own privacy rules, not ours.

Once a card is out, it is out. We cannot see it, change it, or take it back. Anyone who has the picture can open it in the game and get the song, and they can keep it and pass it on. If you post a card in public, anyone can do this. Treat sharing a card the way you would treat posting any other picture.

6. Third-party software

Our games are built with the Unity engine and the Wwise audio engine from Audiokinetic. Both are used purely as on-device software for rendering graphics and playing sound; neither is configured to send data anywhere. Unity Analytics, Unity Ads, Unity in-app purchasing, Unity crash reporting, and Unity performance reporting are all disabled in our builds.

Our games contain no advertising networks, no attribution or measurement SDKs, and no social-media SDKs. They do not display ads and do not offer in-app purchases.

7. What the stores collect

When you download, buy, or update one of our games, the app store handles that transaction under its own privacy policy, not this one — see Apple’s Privacy Policy or Google’s Privacy Policy. The stores provide us with aggregated reports on downloads, regions, and device types. These reports never identify individual users, and we have no way to use them to contact or re-identify anyone.

8. Children’s privacy

Our games carry a 4+ age rating and are suitable for players of all ages, including young children. They are not listed in the App Store Kids Category or in Google Play’s Designed for Families program.

Our games collect no personal information from anyone, and that includes children. We do not collect personal information from children under 13 — not knowingly, and not unknowingly either, because there is no collection at all — as those terms are used in the U.S. Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and comparable laws elsewhere. That covers the persistent identifiers COPPA treats as personal information: our games use none.

If you are a parent or guardian and believe your child has somehow provided us with personal information, please contact us at the address below and we will investigate and delete anything we find.

9. Your privacy rights

Privacy laws such as the California Consumer Privacy Act as amended by the CPRA, the New Jersey Data Privacy Act, the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), and the UK GDPR give you rights to access, correct, delete, or port your personal data, and to object to its sale or sharing.

We honor all of these rights in the simplest possible way: our games give us no personal data about you to begin with, so there is nothing to access, export, correct, or delete on our side, and nothing that could be sold or shared. We have never sold or shared personal information, and we do not use personal information for targeted advertising or automated decision-making. You can remove a game’s locally stored progress at any moment by deleting the game. If you have written to us, you can ask us to delete that correspondence and we will.

We will never charge you, withhold anything, degrade a game, or treat you differently in any way for exercising these rights.

If you would like written confirmation of any of the above for your own records, email us and we will provide it. If you are ever unhappy with how we answer a privacy request, you may reply to ask us to reconsider, and we will respond in writing with our reasoning. You can also complain to your data protection authority — in the EU, your national supervisory authority; in the UK, the Information Commissioner’s Office; in the United States, your state Attorney General.

10. Security and retention

The strongest protection we can offer is not to gather the data in the first place. Since no information ever leaves your device through a game, there is no server, database, or backup of ours that could be breached. Data stored on your device is protected by your device’s own security, including your passcode, your biometric lock, and your operating system’s file encryption.

The one thing we do hold is email you send us. That correspondence stays in our mailbox for as long as it takes to answer you and to keep a record of what we answered, and then we delete it. It goes on no list and is used for nothing else.

11. Changes to this policy

If a future version of any game listed above ever collects data — for example, if we add cloud saves, leaderboards, or in-app purchases — we will update this policy and that game’s App Store privacy disclosures before the version is released, and we will revise the effective date at the top of this page. Material changes will also be noted on this website. Where the law requires your consent for a change — and for anything that would start collecting data about you, it generally does — we will ask you for it and wait for your answer. We will not treat you carrying on playing as consent.

12. Governing law

This policy is governed by the laws of the State of New Jersey and the United States, without regard to conflict-of-law principles. This does not take away any right you have under the data protection laws of the country you live in, and it does not limit those laws where they apply to us.
